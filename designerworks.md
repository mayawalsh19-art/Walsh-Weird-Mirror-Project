1. Daniel Rozin (The Wooden Mirror) 

- Daniel uses a camera to capture the person standing in front of it. A computer then processes that live image and then controls 830 motorized wooden pieces, tilting them to show light and shadow in order to form an image resembling the viewer. 

Input: live video/image of the viewer from a camera 

Output: the wooden tiles move to create an image of the viewer. 

Link: https://smoothware.com/danny/woodenmirror.html 

2. Rafael Lozano-Hemmer and Krzysztof Wodiczko (Zoom Pavilion) 

- The installation uses 12 computerized surveillance systems and face recognition to detect people in the exhibition. Robotic cameras respond by tracking participants and zooming in on them with the resulting live imagery projected across three walls. 

Input: People's faces, positions, and movements detected by cameras. 

Outputs: A constantly changing projected video that tracks and zooms in on participants. 

Link: https://lozano-hemmer.com/zoom_pavilion.php 

3. Mirko Bosetti (Pixellini Sbarazzini) 

- Bosetti turns a static image into thousands of interactive particles. Moving your mouse or using touch causes the pixels to move away from the cursor and then respond through a simulation. 

Input: Mouse movement or touch gestures. 

Output: Image particles move, scatter, warp, and reposition in real time. 

Link: https://pixels.mirkobosetti.com/
